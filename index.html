What matters

Every email is also a Skool post, same day, same copy

“I speedran a Skool from 0 to $231k/month in 30 days” went out from max@kourse.com on 21 July and posted to his free Skool community the same morning. Same for “Alex Hormozi's Skool Strategy for 2026” (27 July), “The NEW Rules of Selling Courses” (9 July) and the rest. One asset, two channels, plus Skool's own weekly digest re-mailing it a third time. He writes once and lands three times.

Every email opens with its own read time

“Read time: 1 minute” is the first line of nearly every send. It removes the only real objection to opening a marketing email, and it is a promise he then has to keep — which keeps the emails genuinely short. worth stealing

The headline number is not rounded

$230,758/month, not “$230k”. Sometimes written $231k in the emails. An exact figure reads as pulled from a dashboard; a round one reads as a claim. Same instinct as PB Trading's “862 days in a row”.

He borrows Hormozi's authority with a receipt, not a name-drop

“I entered Alex Hormozi's Skool Games after building a $230K/month course business in 30 days. Walked away with a $100K check from him personally.” The check is the proof; Hormozi is the witness. Four of the eleven captured emails are built on Hormozi's name.

The offer's second half is the interesting half

The promo is not just 33% off — it is 33% off plus a dedicated 1-on-1 success coach, and one email exists purely to explain why that second part matters more than the discount. He discounts the price and then argues the reader should care about the service instead.

A segmented email that says so out loud

6 July: “Quick one, this email is only going to current Kourse customers, not our full list.” Naming the segment inside the email makes the reader feel selected. Whether the segmentation was real is not verifiable from the inbox.
